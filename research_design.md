# Research Design

## 1. Research Approach

This study will use a **quantitative research approach** to measure consumer preferences and identify patterns in smartphone purchase decisions.

Quantitative data will allow the study to compare the relative importance of different purchase factors and examine relationships between consumer characteristics and purchasing preferences.

---

## 2. Research Type

The study will combine **descriptive and exploratory research**.

### Descriptive Research

The descriptive component will be used to understand:

- Consumer demographic characteristics
- Current smartphone ownership
- Purchasing frequency
- Budget preferences
- Importance of different smartphone attributes
- Brand preferences
- Influence of reviews and recommendations

### Exploratory Research

The exploratory component will investigate potential relationships between consumer characteristics and purchasing behavior, including:

- Income and price sensitivity
- Age and brand preference
- Purchasing frequency and product priorities
- Previous brand experience and future brand preference
- Online reviews and purchase decisions

---

## 3. Research Method

Primary data will be collected through a structured online questionnaire.

The questionnaire will contain primarily closed-ended questions to enable quantitative analysis, along with selected open-ended questions to capture additional consumer perspectives.

---

## 4. Unit of Analysis

The primary unit of analysis will be an **individual consumer** who owns or has previously purchased a smartphone.

Each valid survey response will represent one observation in the dataset.

---

## 5. Target Population

The target population for this study will consist of smartphone users aged **18 years and above** who have purchased or participated in the decision to purchase a smartphone.

The study will aim to include respondents from different demographic and socioeconomic backgrounds in order to capture variation in purchasing preferences.

---

## 6. Data Sources

The study will use two types of data:

### Secondary Data

Secondary information will be collected from credible sources such as:

- Industry research reports
- Market research publications
- Company reports
- Academic research
- Government or institutional sources

Secondary research will be used to establish market context and identify factors that should be investigated through primary research.

### Primary Data

Primary data will be collected directly from consumers using an online questionnaire.

The primary dataset will form the basis for the statistical and exploratory analysis conducted in later stages of the project.

---

## 7. Proposed Sample Size

The study will target approximately **150–250 valid respondents**.

The target is intended to provide sufficient observations for exploratory analysis and comparison between selected consumer segments while remaining feasible for a small-scale independent research project.

The final sample size will depend on the number of valid responses obtained during data collection.

---

## 8. Sampling Method

A **non-probability convenience sampling approach** will initially be used because the study is being conducted as an independent research project with limited access to a predefined population sampling frame.

Respondents may be reached through personal networks, professional networks, educational networks, and online channels.

Where possible, efforts will be made to obtain respondents from different age groups, occupations, income ranges, and geographic backgrounds.

---

## 9. Data Collection Period

The survey will be conducted over a defined collection period.

The exact start date, end date, and number of valid responses will be documented after data collection is completed.

---

## 10. Key Variables

The questionnaire will collect information across the following categories:

### Demographic Variables

- Age group
- Gender
- Occupation
- Income range
- Location

### Smartphone Usage Variables

- Current smartphone brand
- Current smartphone price range
- Smartphone replacement frequency
- Previous smartphone ownership

### Purchase Decision Variables

- Price
- Brand reputation
- Performance
- Camera quality
- Battery life
- Storage
- Display
- Design
- Online reviews
- Recommendations
- Promotional offers
- After-sales service
- Connectivity
- Smart/AI features

### Behavioral Variables

- Purchase frequency
- Budget
- Brand preference
- Purchase intention
- Recommendation behavior
- Importance of online reviews

---

## 11. Measurement Approach

Most purchase-factor variables will be measured using a **5-point Likert scale**.

Example:

| Score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 1 | Not important at all |
| 2 | Slightly important |
| 3 | Moderately important |
| 4 | Very important |
| 5 | Extremely important |

This scale will allow the study to calculate and compare the average importance assigned to different smartphone attributes.

---

## 12. Data Analysis Plan

After data collection, the following analytical approaches will be considered:

### Descriptive Analysis

- Frequency distributions
- Percentages
- Mean
- Median
- Standard deviation
- Cross-tabulations

### Comparative Analysis

Consumer preferences will be compared across selected groups such as:

- Age groups
- Income groups
- Purchase-frequency groups
- Price segments

### Statistical Analysis

Depending on the final structure and measurement scales of the collected data, appropriate statistical techniques may include:

- Chi-square test
- t-test
- ANOVA
- Correlation analysis
- Regression analysis

The final statistical tests will be selected after examining the collected dataset and checking the relevant assumptions.

---

## 13. Data Quality Considerations

Before analysis, responses will be reviewed for:

- Duplicate submissions
- Missing values
- Incomplete responses
- Inconsistent answers
- Extreme or unusual responses
- Potentially invalid responses

Data cleaning decisions will be documented before conducting the final analysis.

---

## 14. Ethical Considerations

Participation in the survey will be voluntary.

Respondents will be informed about the general purpose of the research, and personally identifiable information will not be required unless explicitly necessary.

The collected data will be used only for research and portfolio purposes.

---

## 15. Limitations

The study may have the following limitations:

- Convenience sampling may limit the representativeness of the sample.
- The sample size may not reflect the broader smartphone-user population.
- Survey responses are self-reported and may contain response bias.
- Consumer preferences may vary by geography, culture, and market conditions.
- Findings from this study should therefore be interpreted as exploratory rather than representative of the entire smartphone market.

---

## 16. Expected Outcome

The study is expected to identify the major factors influencing smartphone purchase decisions and determine whether these factors vary across different consumer segments.

The results will be used to develop evidence-based insights and potential recommendations related to product positioning, marketing communication, and customer targeting.
